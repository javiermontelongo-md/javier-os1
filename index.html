What impresses attendings
DO THIS
✓ Pre-round before everyone. Know labs, vitals, events first.
✓ One-liner + assessment + plan. Not a data dump.
✓ Look things up and return with answer same day — always.
✓ Anticipate: "K+ trending down — I already ordered a recheck."
✓ Own mistakes: "I missed that, here's what I'd do differently."
✓ Ask specific feedback: "One thing I should improve?"
✓ Show up to optional teaching even post-call. Noticed.
✓ Flag deteriorating patients early. Flagging is not weakness.
NEVER DO THIS
✗ Come to rounds without knowing overnight events.
✗ Say "I don't know" without following up. The sin is not following up.
✗ Present facts without a plan. Reasoning, not data retrieval.
✗ Phone during rounds — even if others are.
✗ Complain about workload to attendings in first 6 months.
✗ Make the same mistake twice without showing growth.
✗ Disappear after rounds. Attendings notice who is reachable.
✗ Ask generic feedback: "How am I doing?"

Interview preparation
Three questions asked at every anesthesia interview. Practice out loud until they feel natural — not rehearsed.
Q1 — "Why didn't you match anesthesia the first time?"
"My application had real strengths — I interviewed at UCSD, Cedars-Sinai, OHSU, and UC Davis. But I had not yet demonstrated sustained clinical performance in a rigorous environment over time. I chose IM specifically because it would provide exactly that evidence: objective evaluations across complex inpatient and ICU cases. My performance in IM is my answer to that question."

Key: honest, forward-looking, names the gap and closes it immediately.
Q2 — "Why anesthesia — and why now?"
"Internal medicine gave me a longitudinal view of how chronic disease evolves — I now understand patients' trajectories, not just their current numbers. That training made me increasingly drawn to the perioperative window, which represents a physiologically compressed moment where those chronic trajectories become acutely consequential. Anesthesia requires real-time, simultaneous management of interacting organ systems under time pressure — the cognitive environment where I feel most engaged."

Key: no vigilance, no advocacy, no last line of defense. Anesthesia = logical destination of IM reasoning.
Q3 — "What do you bring that others don't?"
"Three things. First, perioperative AKI research — I'm a lead investigator on a study examining surgical AKI risk factors. I understand perioperative physiology at a research level. Second, IM-trained longitudinal physiologic reasoning — most applicants haven't managed the chronic disease progression that creates perioperative risk. I have. Third, operational leadership at scale — $140K fundraising infrastructure, workflow redesign for 300+ families — translates directly into perioperative systems thinking."
